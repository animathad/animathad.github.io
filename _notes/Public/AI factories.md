A working definition, mostly for myself. Expect this to change.

When I say **AI factory**, I don't mean a model, and I don't mean a chatbot bolted onto an IDE. I mean the *production system* around AI: the repeatable pipeline that turns models, data, context, and evaluation into reliable developer value — over and over, safely, at the scale of a whole engineering org.

A factory is the right metaphor because the interesting problems are industrial, not magical:

- **Throughput** — how much useful work moves through, not how impressive a single demo is.
- **Quality control** — evaluation and guardrails as first-class infrastructure, not an afterthought.
- **Standardized parts** — shared context, retrieval, and tooling so every team isn't rebuilding the same jig.
- **Feedback loops** — measuring whether any of this actually made developers faster, and feeding that back in.

The questions I keep circling:

1. What's the *unit of output*? A merged PR? A resolved ticket? Saved developer-hours? You optimize what you measure, so this matters.
2. Where does human judgment stay in the loop, permanently — not as a temporary crutch?
3. How do you keep the platform opinionated enough to be useful but open enough that teams don't route around it?
